A selection of my research on coral reef ecology, thermal stress, coral acclimation, mesophotic ecosystems, coral diseases and environmental change.

RESEARCH HIGHLIGHTS

From coral physiology to reef-scale change

My research combines field observations, transplantation experiments and controlled thermal-stress experiments to investigate how corals respond to environmental change. The studies below represent key stages of this research trajectory.

01
CORAL RESILIENCE • EARLY LIFE STAGES

Adaptations by the coral Acropora tenuis confer resilience to future thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Kitanobo S, Nakamura T, Morita M* (2022). Communications Biology, 5, 1–10.

Research highlight

Heat stress can influence not only the adults that experience it, but also the performance of their offspring. This study found that thermally stressed Acropora tenuis fragments produced more eggs, although the eggs were smaller, while larvae and recruits showed marginally improved survival under elevated temperatures. The results provide evidence that parental thermal history can influence offspring performance and potentially contribute to coral resilience under future warming.

02
CORAL DISEASES • PERSIAN GULF

Baseline assessment of coral diseases in an environmentally extreme environment of the northern Persian Gulf

Hazraty-Kari S*, Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Das RR, Farhadi M, Barkhordari-Ahmadi A, Yahyavi M, Rezai H (2021). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 171, 112707.

Research highlight

This study established a baseline assessment of coral diseases in one of the world's most environmentally extreme coral reef regions. Seven diseases affecting eight scleractinian genera were documented, including the first report of skeletal growth anomaly in Acropora from the Persian Gulf. The dataset provides an important foundation for detecting future changes in coral disease prevalence and reef health.

03
CORAL BLEACHING • EXTREME ENVIRONMENTS

Mass coral bleaching in the northern Persian Gulf, 2012

Kavousi J*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Mohammadizadeh M, Bahrami A, Barkhordari (2014). Scientia Marina, 78(3).

Research highlight

During the 2012 thermal anomaly, approximately 84% of surveyed coral colonies were affected by bleaching. The study estimated a bleaching threshold of approximately 33.5–34 °C for the northern Persian Gulf and showed that even turbid environments, sometimes proposed as potential refugia from warming, did not prevent severe bleaching. The work highlighted the vulnerability of coral communities living near their environmental limits.

04
DEPTH • PHENOTYPIC PLASTICITY

Plasticity of shallow reef corals across a depth gradient

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Hazraty-Kari S, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2023). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 197, 115060.

Research highlight

Shallow corals can respond plastically to major changes in their light environment. Following a one-year depth transplantation experiment, Pocillopora damicornis, Porites cylindrica and Turbinaria reniformis survived and acclimated across depths from 5 to 40 m, whereas Acropora tenuis showed more restricted tolerance. These species-specific responses reveal important limits and opportunities for coral expansion into deeper habitats.

05
THERMAL TOLERANCE • MESOPHOTIC REEFS

Variability in thermal stress thresholds of corals across depths

Tavakoli-Kolour P, Sinniger F, Morita M, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2023). Frontiers in Marine Science, 10, 1210662.

Research highlight

Mesophotic corals experience cooler and often more stable temperatures than shallow corals, but their thermal limits are not necessarily higher. Comparing 11 shallow and mesophotic species revealed that thermal thresholds at depth were generally slightly lower or similar to those of shallow corals. The findings suggest that mesophotic ecosystems may tolerate moderate thermal stress, but their refuge potential may be limited once heat stress becomes severe.

06
JUVENILE CORALS • REPEATED HEAT STRESS

Reactions of juvenile coral to three years of consecutive thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2023). Science of the Total Environment, 866, 161227.

Research highlight

Repeated heatwaves do not necessarily produce increasing resilience. In a three-year experiment, Acropora tenuis juveniles experienced a short period of heat stress each year. Survival remained above 96% during the first heat exposure but declined to approximately 50% after repeated stress, accompanied by reductions in symbiont density and photosynthetic efficiency. The study demonstrates that repeated thermal stress can progressively erode resilience during early coral life.

07
MESOPHOTIC REEFS • ACCLIMATION

Acclimation potential of Acropora to mesophotic environment

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Harii S* (2023). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 188, 114698.

Research highlight

Early life stages of shallow-water corals can show substantial plasticity when transplanted into deeper environments. Four Acropora species were transplanted from shallow reefs to depths of 5–40 m. Their survival and growth revealed strong species-specific responses, with some species showing particularly high performance at 40 m. These results highlight both the potential and biological limits of mesophotic habitats as future coral refugia.

08
CORAL LARVAE • THERMAL ACCLIMATION

Response of resistant larvae of the coral Acropora tenuis to future thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Harii S* (2023). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 192, 115060.

Research highlight

Thermal exposure during the larval stage did not translate into increased thermal tolerance later in juvenile life. Larvae exposed to elevated temperatures showed altered settlement responses, but juveniles subsequently exposed to heat did not demonstrate improved survival. The findings suggest that thermal resistance in coral larvae does not necessarily generate a persistent memory of heat stress across life stages.

09
THERMAL MEMORY • CORAL PHYSIOLOGY

Susceptibility of Acropora tenuis to consecutive thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Nakamura T, Morita M* (2024). Coral Reefs, 43, 4.

Research highlight

Repeated bleaching-level heat stress can make adult corals increasingly vulnerable rather than more resistant. Across three consecutive years, Acropora tenuis fragments exposed to repeated thermal stress showed declining survival, reduced photosynthetic efficiency and lower symbiotic algal density. Survival under stress ultimately declined to approximately 20%, revealing important limits to coral acclimatization under recurring heatwaves.

10
MESOPHOTIC REFUGIA • THERMAL TOLERANCE

Shallow corals acclimate to mesophotic depths while maintaining their heat tolerance

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Hazraty-Kari S, Nakamura T, Harii S* (2024). Marine Pollution Bulletin, 209, 117277.

Research highlight

Corals transplanted from shallow reefs to 40 m for one year retained substantial thermal tolerance despite prolonged exposure to cooler, low-light conditions. Mesophotic-acclimated fragments showed higher thermal thresholds, delayed bleaching and better maintenance of photosynthetic efficiency than shallow fragments. The findings support the potential of mesophotic environments to function as refugia while revealing the importance of low light as a regulator of coral responses to heat.

11
CORAL DISEASES • PERSIAN GULF

Outbreak of growth anomalies in coral communities of Qeshm Island, Persian Gulf

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Kavousi J, Rezai H (2015). International Aquatic Research, 7, 151–156.

Research highlight

This study documented an outbreak of growth anomalies in coral communities of Qeshm Island. Growth anomalies affected substantial proportions of Porites and Platygyra daedalea colonies and were strongly associated with colony size. The work provided early evidence of disease and health threats affecting corals in the environmentally extreme Persian Gulf.

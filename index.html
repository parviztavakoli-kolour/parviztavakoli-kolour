Shallow corals acclimate to mesophotic depths while maintaining their heat tolerance against ongoing climate change

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Hazraty-Kari S, Nakamura T, Harii S*

Marine Pollution Bulletin (2024), 209: 117277

Key findings
  • Shallow corals retained heat tolerance after transplantation to mesophotic depths.
  • Mesophotic-acclimated corals showed higher thermal thresholds.
  • Low light intensity moderated bleaching responses.
  • Mesophotic ecosystems may provide refugia for shallow corals in warming oceans.
Abstract

Global warming poses a significant threat to coral reefs. It has been assumed that mesophotic coral ecosystems may serve as refugia from ocean warming. This study examined the acclimation capacity and thermal tolerance of two shallow coral species, Porites cylindrica and Turbinaria reniformis, transplanted to mesophotic depths of 40 m for 12 months. Mesophotic-acclimated fragments showed higher thermal thresholds and survival rates, delayed onset of bleaching, and less decline in photosynthetic efficiency compared with shallow fragments.

Susceptibility of Acropora tenuis to consecutive thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Nakamura T, Morita M*

Coral Reefs (2024), 43:4

Key findings
  • Three years of repeated thermal stress progressively reduced coral survival.
  • Survival under thermal stress declined to approximately 20%.
  • Photosynthetic efficiency and symbiont density decreased under repeated stress.
  • Consecutive bleaching-level heat stress increased coral susceptibility.
Abstract

Elevated temperatures cause mass coral bleaching and reef degradation. This study investigated how three years of consecutive thermal stress affected adult fragments of Acropora tenuis. Fragments were exposed to approximately 28 °C control conditions and approximately 31 °C heat stress. Survival, maximum quantum yield of photosystem II and algal density were measured. Survival under thermal stress decreased over the three-year period, reaching 20% by the end. Consecutive bleaching-level thermal stress therefore increased coral susceptibility to heat.

Plasticity of shallow reef corals across a depth gradient

Tavakoli-Kolour P*, Sinniger F, Morita M, Hazraty-Kari S, Nakamura T, Harii S*

Marine Pollution Bulletin (2023), 197: 115060

Key findings
  • Coral species used different photoacclimation strategies across depths.
  • Species showed distinct morphological plasticity.
  • Depth strongly affected physiological performance and habitat extension.
  • Porites cylindrica and Turbinaria reniformis showed strong acclimation capacity.
Abstract

Mesophotic coral ecosystems have been suggested as potential refugia for shallow reefs. We investigated the photoacclimation potential of four shallow coral species transplanted across different depths for one year. Several species survived and acclimated to a wide range of light regimes at 5, 20 and 40 m, while Acropora tenuis survived only at 5 and 20 m. The results demonstrate substantial plasticity in shallow corals exposed to depth changes.

Variability in thermal stress thresholds of corals across depths

Tavakoli-Kolour P, Sinniger F, Morita M, Nakamura T, Harii S*

Frontiers in Marine Science (2023), 10: 1210662

Key findings
  • Thermal thresholds varied among coral species and depths.
  • Mesophotic corals generally had slightly lower or similar thresholds to shallow corals.
  • Mesophotic corals can tolerate relatively low levels of thermal stress.
  • Higher accumulated heat stress can still result in bleaching and mortality.
Abstract

Mesophotic habitats are generally cooler than shallow habitats, but the thermal susceptibility of mesophotic corals remains poorly understood. We compared mesophotic and shallow coral species using physiological responses to thermal stress. Coral fragments were exposed to approximately 30 and 31 °C treatments and a control of approximately 28 °C for 14 days. Thermal thresholds were slightly lower or equal in mesophotic species, suggesting that mesophotic corals can survive relatively low levels of thermal stress but may experience bleaching and mortality under greater heat accumulation.

Reactions of juvenile coral to three years of consecutive thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Nakamura T, Morita M

Science of the Total Environment (2023), 866: 161227

Key findings
  • Repeated annual heat stress progressively reduced juvenile survival.
  • Survival declined to approximately 50% after three years of stress.
  • Symbiotic algal density and photosynthetic efficiency decreased under stress.
  • Young A. tenuis did not acclimatize effectively to repeated heatwaves.
Abstract

Coral juveniles were exposed to control temperatures of approximately 26–28 °C and heat stress of approximately 31 °C for one week per year over three consecutive years. More than 96% survived the first year of heat stress, whereas survival declined substantially by the third year. Symbiotic algal density and photosynthetic activity also decreased under stress. The results demonstrate that repeated thermal stress can progressively reduce juvenile coral resilience.

Response of resistant larvae of the coral Acropora tenuis to future thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Harii S*

Marine Pollution Bulletin (2023), 192: 115060

Key findings
  • Larval thermal stress negatively affected settlement.
  • Thermal conditioning during the larval stage did not increase juvenile heat tolerance.
  • Thermal-resistant larvae could not maintain enhanced tolerance into the juvenile stage.
  • Heatwaves may threaten resilience during early coral life stages.
Abstract

Thermal conditioning during early life stages may influence coral thermal tolerance. Resistant larvae of Acropora tenuis were exposed to ambient and thermal-stress temperatures and subsequently assessed for settlement and juvenile survival. Thermal stress during the larval stage did not increase the thermal tolerance of juveniles, indicating limited carryover acclimation across this life-stage transition.

Acclimation potential of Acropora to mesophotic environment

Tavakoli-Kolour P, Sinniger F, Morita M, Harii S*

Marine Pollution Bulletin (2023), 188: 114698

Key findings
  • Four shallow Acropora species showed different responses to depth.
  • Survival at deeper habitats varied strongly among species.
  • Juvenile morphology changed across depth gradients.
  • Some shallow coral species may extend their habitat into cooler deeper environments.
Abstract

Mesophotic coral ecosystems may provide refuge from climate change. This study investigated the acclimation potential of four shallow Acropora species by transplanting larvae and early polyps to 5, 10, 20 and 40 m depths. Survival, growth, size and morphological characteristics differed among species and depths. The results demonstrate substantial plasticity in early life stages of shallow corals across depth gradients.

Adaptations by the coral Acropora tenuis confer resilience to future thermal stress

Hazraty-Kari S*, Morita M, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Kitanobo S, Nakamura T, Morita M*

Communications Biology (2022), 5: 1–10

Key findings
  • Heat-stressed colonies increased egg production while producing smaller eggs.
  • Offspring from stressed colonies showed marginally higher survival under elevated temperature.
  • Thermal stress can influence reproductive investment and offspring performance.
  • Corals may transfer aspects of thermal resilience across generations.
Abstract

Coral may have strategies that improve survival under warming conditions by producing more heat-tolerant offspring. Fragments of the reef-building coral Acropora tenuis were exposed to control or heat-stress temperatures for ten days. Heat-stressed fragments showed a trade-off in egg production, producing more but smaller eggs. Larvae and recruits from stressed colonies showed marginally higher survival under elevated temperature. These responses may contribute to coral reef resilience under increasing thermal stress.

International Scientific Assessment & Policy Report

World Ocean Assessment III — Tropical and Subtropical Coral Reefs

Sweet MJ, Beger M, Benzoni F, Camp E, del Campo J, Dobson KL, Galvis N, Ghodrati Shojaei M, Goby G, Harding S, Huang D, Ivanenko V, Richards Z, Rinkevich B, Schläppy ML, Storlazzi CD, Tavakoli-Kolour P, Karenne T, van der Meij S, Villalobos J, Voolstra CR, Walker BK (2026).

Subchapter 5d: Tropical and Subtropical Coral Reefs. World Ocean Assessment III, United Nations, New York, USA, pp. 325–344.

Key points

  • Global declines in coral cover persist due to ocean warming, coastal development, pollution, sedimentation, disease and extraction of marine resources.
  • The frequency and severity of heatwaves, disease outbreaks and tropical storms have increased, reducing recovery time between disturbances.
  • Coral reefs provide major ecological, economic, coastal-protection and livelihood benefits.
  • Conservation and restoration require long-term, sustainable and evidence-based approaches that consider socioeconomic conditions.
  • Global efforts to reduce emissions and climate change are essential for maintaining coral reef ecosystem function.
  • Shifting baselines need to be considered when defining objectives for coral reef conservation, restoration and regeneration.

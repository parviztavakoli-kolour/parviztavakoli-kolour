About Me

Researching coral reefs across environmental gradients, life stages, and scales — from individual coral physiology to reef-scale community change.

I am a marine scientist and coral reef ecologist interested in understanding how corals cope with environmental variability and increasing thermal stress under climate change.

My research combines field observations, controlled experiments, ecological surveys, and quantitative approaches to investigate coral acclimation, adaptation, thermal tolerance, bleaching, mortality, and reef resilience.

My work spans tropical coral reef systems in the Indo-Pacific and Persian Gulf, allowing me to investigate how different thermal environments shape coral responses to climate extremes.

Research

My research focuses on understanding why some corals survive environmental extremes while others bleach, suffer mortality, or fail to recover.

Coral Thermal Tolerance

Understanding variation in coral heat tolerance and the mechanisms underlying resistance to marine heatwaves and thermal stress.

Acclimation & Adaptation

Investigating how corals respond to environmental conditions across depths, habitats, latitudinal gradients, and generations.

Environmental Memory

Exploring whether previous thermal exposure can prime corals for future heatwaves and whether this thermal memory has measurable limits.

Coral Bleaching & Mortality

Identifying ecological and colony-level factors that explain variation in bleaching severity, mortality, and recovery.

Mesophotic Coral Reefs

Investigating whether deeper coral populations can provide thermal refugia and contribute to coral reef persistence under climate change.

Reef Community Change

Examining long-term changes in coral communities and the ecological consequences of repeated thermal disturbances.

Current Research Directions

Selected research themes and experimental directions that currently shape my work.

Environmental Memory and the Limits of Coral Thermal Priming

How long can corals retain the benefits of previous thermal exposure? This research explores whether thermal variability and repeated heatwaves generate persistent physiological memory, and whether this memory eventually weakens or becomes insufficient under increasingly severe marine heatwaves.

Coral Responses Across Thermal Environments

Comparing corals originating from thermally stable and highly variable environments to understand how environmental history influences heat tolerance, bleaching susceptibility, and recovery.

Climate-Driven Changes in Coral Reef Communities

Combining long-term ecological observations with environmental datasets to investigate how repeated climate extremes reshape coral communities across geographical and environmental gradients.

About Me

Researching coral reefs across environmental gradients, life stages, and ecological scales.

I am a marine scientist and coral reef ecologist interested in understanding how corals respond to environmental variability and increasing thermal stress under climate change.

My research combines field observations, ecological surveys, controlled experiments, environmental monitoring, and quantitative approaches to investigate coral acclimation, adaptation, thermal tolerance, bleaching, mortality, and reef resilience.

My work spans tropical coral reef systems across the Indo-Pacific and Persian Gulf, providing an opportunity to examine coral responses across contrasting environmental conditions.

Research

My research focuses on understanding why some corals tolerate environmental extremes while others bleach, experience mortality, or fail to recover.

Coral Thermal Tolerance

Understanding variation in coral heat tolerance and the mechanisms underlying resistance to marine heatwaves and thermal stress.

Acclimation & Adaptation

Investigating how corals respond to environmental conditions across depths, habitats, geographical gradients, and life stages.

Coral Bleaching & Mortality

Identifying ecological and colony-level factors that explain variation in bleaching severity, mortality, and recovery.

Mesophotic Coral Reefs

Investigating whether deeper coral populations can provide thermal refugia and contribute to coral reef persistence under climate change.

Coral Reproduction & Life History

Exploring coral reproduction, early life stages, larval ecology, and how environmental conditions influence coral population persistence.

Reef Community Change

Examining long-term changes in coral communities and the ecological consequences of repeated environmental disturbances.
